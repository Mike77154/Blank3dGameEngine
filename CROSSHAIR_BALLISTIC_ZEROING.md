# Crosshair convergence and ballistic zeroing — v3.6.3

## Problem fixed

The old weapon pose generated two parallel rays:

```text
camera/HUD ray  ---------------------------->
muzzle ray      ---------------------------->
```

In FPS this could look close enough, but in over-the-shoulder view the muzzle is
laterally and vertically displaced from the camera. Parallel rays never meet,
so a physical projectile could pass beside the point marked by the HUD.

## New provider flow

```text
CamaraNaku camera provider
        |
        v
camera origin + center HUD forward
        |
        v
spread provider (per pellet)
        |
        v
CCS/SICOL raycast provider
        |
        +-- hit: exact hit point
        |
        +-- no hit: far point on HUD ray
        |
        v
muzzle origin -> target point
        |
        v
Gamlib3D math provider normalizes launch direction
        |
        v
physical projectile spawn
```

The raycast remains camera-owned, but the physical projectile remains
muzzle-owned. This removes parallax without turning the projectile into hitscan.
An obstacle between the muzzle and target can still intercept the projectile.

## Gravity and Bolt3D zeroing

Linear bullets, pellets, rockets, Magnum, sniper and Gatling rounds use the exact
normalized muzzle-to-target vector.

Gravity-driven recipes pass through `blank3d_ballistics_resolve_launch()`:

1. Read the target point generated by the HUD ray.
2. Read the real projectile speed and recipe gravity.
3. Estimate flight time in Q20.12.
4. Solve a low launch arc iteratively without float/double.
5. Launch with the same speed used by the physics backend.

The grenade no longer uses one gravity value for aiming and another hardcoded
value for flight. The `Blank3DWeaponModules.gravity_q16` recipe drives both.
Bolt3D projectiles receive the same gravity compensation before their provider
adds drag, bounce and collision.

## Shotgun behavior

Each pellet first receives its normal cone spread around the center HUD ray.
Each spread ray resolves its own target point, then converges from the common
muzzle. The shotgun therefore keeps intentional dispersion while the pattern as
a whole remains centered on the reticle.

## Added validation

`make test-aim-convergence` verifies:

- a muzzle displaced from the camera converges on the HUD ray;
- the no-hit target is generated from camera origin, not muzzle origin;
- emitted direction is normalized muzzle-to-target;
- a gravity projectile receives upward zeroing compensation;
- the compensated trajectory reaches within one world unit of the HUD target.
